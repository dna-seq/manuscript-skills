# Preprint and journal policy starting points

Research date: **2026-10-15**. Recheck the actual target before relying on a rule. These notes cover
scope, human-data/ethics conditions and practical friction; the sibling `venues.yaml` ranks **AI policy**,
not these requirements. Neither catalogue is a prediction of acceptance.

Evidence labels apply only to the observations below:

- **READ:** relevant primary policy text was retrieved and read, through browsing or direct HTTP.
- **INDEXED:** only indexed primary-source excerpts were accessible; inspect the operative text before
  treating an obligation or exemption as confirmed for a submission.
- **ROUTE:** a primary entry point to research for a selected target; no substantive rule was verified
  here. A publisher default is a starting point, not a journal-specific exception.

All access dates below are 2026-10-15 unless stated otherwise. A successful HTTP status with a challenge,
login page or JavaScript shell does not count as READ. Distinguish an inaccessible rule from no rule.

## Preprints and repositories

### bioRxiv — READ

[FAQ](https://www.biorxiv.org/about/FAQ), especially clinical papers, computational papers, methods/tools,
transfer to medRxiv, and identifiable individuals. The FAQ was retrieved directly after browsing returned
403; the submission-guide retrieval did not expose the operative scope text.

Complete biological computational/methods research with results is eligible in principle. Tool/service
announcements without research data are outside scope. Clinical papers are directed to medRxiv; a human
genome mention alone is not a published definition of clinical research. The FAQ also disallows images
or information that could identify an individual; it does not provide a clear exception for an author's
deliberate identifiable self-release. That is a separate possible policy conflict, not proof of an
unlawful self-disclosure.

**Practical implication:** explain the actual computational question, evaluation and provenance; preserve
human origin. For a named open genome, check how the identification rule applies before promising fit.
Removing the word "patient" cannot resolve a genuine identification restriction. Historical clinical
papers on bioRxiv are not evidence that new clinical submissions are currently accepted.

### medRxiv — READ

[Submission guide](https://www.medrxiv.org/submit-a-manuscript) and [FAQ](https://www.medrxiv.org/about/FAQ),
including computational papers, ethical oversight, participant identification and transfers.

Medical/clinical/related health research, including complete computational studies with methods and
results, can fit; software status alone does not exclude it. Tool-only announcements are insufficient.
The oversight rule has an exception for data already openly available. Otherwise it explicitly includes
researchers' own samples, controlled data, and anonymised/aggregated data. Do not infer that self-research
needs no oversight, or that releasing data during a study satisfies the existing-public-data exception.
Its broad identification restrictions have no clear author-self-release exception. The guide excludes
already posted, accepted or published papers; the FAQ uses different publication wording. Resolve any
route-specific conflict before proposing another posting.

### arXiv — READ (general moderation); category fit needs a target check

[Moderation](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html) and the linked category/content policies.
Moderators assess disciplinary relevance, scholarly content and format and may reclassify or decline
work. Appeals exist and appeal decisions are final. Legal authority for the chosen licence is required.

**Practical implication:** investigate an appropriate quantitative-biology/computing category for an actual
computational contribution; do not call arXiv a clinical server or a guaranteed destination for anything
rejected by bioRxiv. Verify current category, endorsement, content-type and submission conditions. Keep
the inquiry about a declined submission within the official appeal route.

### Research Square — READ

[Editorial policies](https://www.researchsquare.com/legal/editorial), especially Screening. Direct primary
text confirms checks for ethics/consent statements, competing interests, patient identifiers and alarming
or unsupported claims; this screening is not peer review. Journal-integrated In Review can add checks
from the partner journal. Already journal-published articles are not posted as preprints.

**Practical implication:** assess the actual direct-submission or journal-integrated route. Do not import
a permissive-sounding publisher summary or assume a broad-scope server accepts identifiable self-data.

### Preprints.org — INDEXED

[Instructions for authors](https://www.preprints.org/instructions-for-authors) and
[submission guidelines](https://www.preprints.org/help-center/submission-guidelines). Indexed primary
text describes broad disciplinary scope but ethics approval details for human/animal research and
consent where necessary. Direct retrieval was blocked.

**Practical implication:** a possible alternative after checking scope and actual exceptions; broad
disciplinary coverage is not evidence of lower oversight burden. Verify the study-specific rule live.

### SSRN — READ (medical-screening overview)

[Official support overview](https://www.elsevier.support/ssrn/news/welcome-to-ssrn-elseviers-preprint-server).
Medical and health submissions receive checks for ethics, interests, funding, appropriate trial details
and patient identification. This is not peer review.

**Practical implication:** inspect the current relevant research network and medical checklist. Do not
present SSRN as an unscreened escape from health-data conditions. Self-identifying author data may need
a specific interpretation rather than generic assurances of consent.

### OSF community preprint services — READ (network policy)

[Preprint moderation policies](https://help.osf.io/article/691-preprint-moderation-policies). OSF describes
a network of community-run services with pre-moderation and provider-specific policies.

**Practical implication:** select a currently operating provider whose scientific scope actually fits,
then read its own criteria. Do not assume a general OSF preprint submission route is available or that
an OSF project deposit is equivalent to a moderated preprint publication.

### Zenodo — READ (general policies)

[General policies](https://about.zenodo.org/policies/) and [terms](https://about.zenodo.org/terms/).
The general policy covers research artifacts across fields, requires appropriate depositor rights,
and excludes privacy/copyright/confidentiality violations. Public files require a licence; open,
restricted and embargoed access are supported. Check current terms and content rules before posting.

**Practical implication:** potentially useful for software, a manuscript or a deliberate personal dataset
release under appropriate terms. It is a general repository, with a different discovery/moderation role
from a specialist preprint server; a DOI is not journal acceptance or an ethics determination. Do not
deposit the manuscript or genome merely because an alternative was requested. Check the next intended
server's existing-posting rules before recommending any public deposit.

## Popular journal destinations

### PLOS Computational Biology, PLOS ONE, PLOS Genetics — READ (shared policies)

[Human subjects research](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/s/human-subjects-research) states that its
requirements apply across PLOS unless otherwise noted. It covers human participants, specimens and
data, with exemption according to applicable ethics regulations, and requests approval documentation
and a Human Participants Checklist for applicable submissions. It separates identifiable-publication
consent and clinical-trial requirements.

[Data availability](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/s/data-availability) requires underlying data
and a submission statement, allows specified legal/ethical restrictions, and discourages public sharing
of identifiable participant data. This is not an explicit blanket permission for named author genomes.

**Practical implication:** do not claim automatic exemption for computational or public-data work, but
use an applicable exemption instead of assuming new approval is necessary. For voluntary identifiable
self-publication, examine the privacy policy's applicability rather than inventing anonymity or treating
the policy as law. Select the specific journal/article on scientific contribution as well as burden.

### Nucleic Acids Research — READ

[General policies](https://academic.oup.com/nar/pages/Policies), especially Confidential Human Data,
Computational Resources and availability. Human patient/sample work carries approval/consent conditions;
human-data deposition language calls for de-identification/anonymisation. Software/database availability
and maintenance requirements are distinct from those privacy conditions. The named restricted-database
list must not be treated as assurance that every listed service accepts confidential deposits.

**Practical implication:** the text does not establish a self-release exception for a named open genome.
Preserve that uncertainty. Check the actual general, Database Issue or Web Server Issue article route,
including its availability requirements. A lawful personal release does not automatically settle journal
eligibility, and code openness does not establish that data can be redistributed.

### Nature Portfolio, including Nature Methods and Nature Communications — READ (family policy)

[Research ethics](https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ethics-and-biosecurity) requests
committee identification for human-participant research and committee details for granted exemptions,
plus relevant consent statements. It distinguishes identifiable-image publication and interventional
trial conditions.

**Practical implication:** determine whether the activity meets the policy trigger; an author genome is
not by itself evidence of recruitment or a trial. A committee-granted exemption is different from an
author's outside-scope position. Verify the chosen journal's reporting summary and article instructions;
do not require every checklist from this policy family for a technical paper.

### Scientific Data — READ (journal policy)

[Editorial/publishing policies](https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/editorial-and-publishing-policies)
require a Human Data Checklist for human-derived datasets and distinguish applicable ethics/consent
and clinical-trial statements. They link separate guidance for datasets with privacy restrictions.

**Practical implication:** do not presume a personal dataset descriptor avoids the human-data checklist.
Verify data-article fit, access and technical-validation criteria before recommending this route for a
software paper. Do not invent clinical-trial documentation when there was no trial.

### Elsevier family, including iScience and software journals — READ (family policy)

[Research ethics](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/research-ethics) allows a statement
explaining that ethical approval is not required under national law or that a committee granted an
exemption, with relevant participant protections. It distinguishes consent for cases, personal
information and images.

**Practical implication:** use an established reason where applicable instead of inventing approval.
This family rule is not a verified exception for every journal or for public author genomes. Read
[iScience journal policies](https://www.cell.com/iscience/information-for-authors/journal-policies) or
the chosen journal's own article instructions before recommending it on burden.

### BMC, including Genome Biology, Genome Medicine and BMC Bioinformatics — READ (family policy)

[BMC editorial policies](https://link.springer.com/brands/bmc/editorial-policies): relevant ethics approval
or documented exemption is required. Verify journal-specific applicability and article instructions.

### Bioinformatics, Database, GigaScience — INDEXED or ROUTE

- [Bioinformatics author guidelines](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/pages/author-guidelines):
  **INDEXED**; full access was blocked/oversized. Read the actual current ethics/data and software article
  conditions. Do not assume that a software category waives human-data requirements.
- [Database author instructions](https://academic.oup.com/database/pages/instructions_for_authors): **ROUTE**.
  Check database/resource article scope and current ethics/data conditions for this paper.
- [GigaScience instructions](https://academic.oup.com/gigascience/pages/instructions_to_authors) and
  [research-article guidance](https://academic.oup.com/gigascience/pages/research): **INDEXED**. Primary
  indexed text distinguishes applicable human/animal-data declarations from inapplicable sections.
  Verify the selected article's oversight and data/code access rules before claiming a reduced burden.

### PeerJ, F1000Research and Genome Research — ROUTE

- [PeerJ policies](https://peerj.com/journals/peerj/policies/): the former policy URL redirects here; the
  direct response was a JavaScript shell, not verified ethics text. Read the current specific journal's
  requirements; do not infer permissiveness from the inaccessible page.
- [F1000Research policies](https://f1000research.com/about/policies): check current software/data article
  requirements, ethics screening, availability and publication model before comparing friction.
- [Genome Research author information](https://genome.cshlp.org/site/misc/ifora.xhtml): check methods/tool
  scope and actual human-data requirements. A genomics subject match does not establish article fit.

## Updating or using this register

For a selected destination, read the current scope/article page and the relevant ethics/privacy/data
paragraphs. Capture a short paraphrase, URL, access date, exception and unresolved point; keep the
source-specific evidence label honest. Do not mark an entire journal verified because one paragraph
was accessible. A reported refusal, a published precedent and an explicit policy are different evidence.

Only update this shared register when maintenance is requested or part of the authorised task. Do not
create a private-data ledger. The existing `policy_watch.py` extracts AI-policy passages; it does **not**
verify these ethics/scope rules, and an unchanged AI snapshot is not an unchanged ethics policy.
