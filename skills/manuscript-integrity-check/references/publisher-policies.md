## 1. Cross-publisher bodies

### 1.1 ICMJE Recommendations, updated January 2026 (new Section V) — VERIFIED

- URL: https://www.icmje.org/icmje-recommendations.pdf (header: "Updated January 2026"); news: https://www.icmje.org/news-and-editorials/updated_recommendations_jan2026.html
- The news page confirms "two new sections addressing expanded guidance on the use of AI in publishing (new Section V) and authors' access to data (new Section III.L.2)". Section V has V.A (authors), V.B (reviewers), V.C (editors). (The news page's HTML title says "January 2025"; the heading and body say January 2026.)
- Comparison with the archived April 2025 PDF: the Methods sentence on "tool used, version, and prompts where applicable" and the "Referencing AI-generated material as the primary source is not acceptable" sentence already existed in April 2025. **New in January 2026**: the consolidated Section V, and the nondisclosure-as-misconduct sentence, the spell-check example, and "Editors should be vigilant" (absent from the April 2025 text).

Verbatim, Section V preamble:
> "Because AI can generate authoritative-sounding output that can be incorrect, incomplete, or biased, humans are ultimately responsible for reviewing and ensuring the accuracy of any content generated with assistance of AI."
> "• Authors should not list or cite AI and AI-assisted technologies as an author."
> "• Because AI tools may incorporate information from sources without proper attribution (including copyright material), humans are responsible for citing appropriate sources, obtaining permissions when necessary, and ensuring that plagiarism has not occurred when using content generated by AI tools."
> "• Editors/publishers, authors, and reviewers should be transparent in their use of AI tools at any stage of the editorial process, including manuscript preparation, editing, and review. When AI is used, users should disclose which tool was used, and for what purpose."

V.A Use of AI by Authors:
> "Authors who use such technology should describe, in both the cover letter and the submitted work in the appropriate section if applicable, how they used it"
> "Authors should be able to assert that there is no plagiarism in their paper, including in text and images produced by the AI. Humans must ensure there is appropriate attribution of all quoted material, including full citations. Referencing AI-generated material as the primary source is not acceptable."
> "Nondisclosure of AI use may require corrective action and may be construed as misconduct in some circumstances (see Sections III.A and III.B)"

V.C (editors):
> "For example, using AI to spell-check and correct grammar is generally viewed as acceptable whereas using AI for content generation must be considered in the context of the work. In all use cases, authors should check the accuracy and validity of their content before submission. Editors should be vigilant that authors may not disclose AI use when queried."

II.A.4 (where to disclose):
> "For example, if AI was used for writing assistance, describe this in the acknowledgment section (see Section II.A.3). If AI was used for data collection, analysis, or figure generation, authors should describe this use in the methods (see Section IV.A.3.d)."

IV.A.3.d (Methods):
> "Authors who used AI technology to conduct the study should describe its use in the Methods section in sufficient detail to enable replication of the approach, including the tool used, version, and prompts where applicable."

References section:
> "Referencing AI-generated material as the primary source is not acceptable. Authors are responsible for citing references accurately and should be able to attest that the references cited support the associated statement. To minimize bibliographic citation errors, references should be verified using either a bibliographic source, such as PubMed, or original sources. Authors are responsible for checking that none of the references cite retracted articles except in the context of referring to the retraction."

Triggers: nondisclosure → "corrective action", possibly misconduct (III.A/III.B). ICMJE sets norms; journals enforce.

### 1.2 COPE — VERIFIED

**Position statement "Authorship and AI tools"** — https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/authorship-and-ai-tools
- Page shows "Last reviewed: 13 February 2023"; change history "20 August 2024 Version 1 published"; © 2024. **No 2025–2026 revision** of the statement.
> "AI tools cannot meet the requirements for authorship as they cannot take responsibility for the submitted work. As non-legal entities, they cannot assert the presence or absence of conflicts of interest nor manage copyright and license agreements."
> "Authors who use AI tools in the writing of a manuscript, production of images or graphical elements of the paper, or in the collection and analysis of data, must be transparent in disclosing in the Materials and Methods (or similar section) of the paper how the AI tool was used and which tool was used. Authors are fully responsible for the content of their manuscript, even those parts produced by an AI tool, and are thus liable for any breach of publication ethics."

**2026 activity: COPE case "Editors suspect that reviewers are using artificial intelligence"** — https://publicationethics.org/guidance/case/editors-suspect-reviewers-are-using-artificial-intelligence — "Advice was originally provided by COPE Council, and was updated following advice from Members at the Forum in March 2026." Case advice, "not formal COPE guidance".
> "Participants noted several other possible flags for generative AI use in review reports, including extensive comments returned in short timeframes, use of bullet points and dashes, circular reasoning, and a lack of engagement with the academic value and context of the article. While editors should not accuse reviewers of using AI without proof, they can discard a review if they are not convinced of its value."
> "Reviewers should not be allowed to upload manuscripts to AI tools as this can violate confidentiality and infringe on the author's exclusive rights to the manuscript and its content."

COPE is also a partner in the Global Reporting Standard for AI Disclosure (§1.6).

### 1.3 WAME — VERIFIED (no 2024–2026 revision found)

- URL: https://wame.org/page3.php?id=106 — "Chatbots, Generative AI, and Scholarly Manuscripts", "Revised May 31, 2023".
> "WAME Recommendations 2.1: Authors submitting a paper in which a chatbot/AI was used to draft new text should note such use in the acknowledgment; all prompts used to generate new text, or to convert text or text prompts into tables or illustrations, should be specified."
> "WAME Recommendation 2.2: When an AI tool such as a chatbot is used to carry out or generate analytical work, help report results (e.g., generating tables or figures), or write computer codes, this should be stated in the body of the paper, in both the Abstract and the Methods section. In the interests of enabling scientific scrutiny, including replication and identifying falsification, the full prompt used to generate the research results, the time and date of query, and the AI tool used and its version, should be provided."
> "Similarly, authors must ensure that all quoted material is appropriately attributed, including full citations, and that the cited sources support the chatbot's statements."

WAME is the strictest body on prompts: all prompts for drafted text, plus prompt, date/time and version for analysis or code, stated in the Abstract and the Methods.

### 1.4 STM — VERIFIED

**Recommendations for a Classification of AI Use in Academic Manuscript Preparation**, September 2025. Page: https://www.stm-assoc.org/document/recommendations-for-a-classification-of-ai-use-in-academic-manuscript-preparation/ (published 22 Sep 2025); PDF: https://s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/stm.offloadmedia/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/23020709/STM_AI_Classification_Recs_19_Sept2025-1.pdf
- Nine classes: (1) refinement/correction/editing for language clarity; (2) writing or drafting manuscript content; (3) translation; (4) refining/formatting data reported; (5) generating/editing images, diagrams or figures for illustrative purposes only; (6) generating/refining visualisations of research data or results; (7) refining/formatting code reported (without altering functionality); (8) assisting with gathering references; (9) "Presentation of any kind of content generated by AI tools as though it were original research".
- Class 8 explicitly excludes "using AI tools to generate plausible-sounding references that do not exist".
> "We further recommend that policies and guidance for authors clearly specify the required level of disclosure – for example, whether particular tools or technologies, or the exact prompts used with generative AI tools, must be explicitly reported."
- OUP's July 2026 journal policy points authors to this classification.

**STM Integrity Hub** — https://stm-assoc.org/what-we-do/strategic-areas/research-integrity/integrity-hub/ — VERIFIED that it exists and integrates Springer Nature's AI-text tool (formerly "Geppetto", donated 30 April 2025: https://group.springernature.com/gp/group/media/press-releases/research-integrity-ai-tool-donation/27774818), Clear Skies' Papermill Alarm, PubPeer data, and a paper-mill checker.
> Springer Nature release: "The tool has already been responsible for identifying hundreds of fake papers soon after submission".
- No dated 2026 Integrity Hub announcement found: **NOT FOUND**.

## 2. Oxford University Press

### 2.1 OUP "Policy on AI Use & Disclosure for OUP Journals Authors" — VERIFIED (key new policy)

- URL: https://academic.oup.com/pages/for-authors/journals/preparing-and-submitting-your-manuscript/ai-principles-for-oxford-journals/policy-on-ai-use-and-disclosure-for-oxford-journals-authors
- "Policy last updated on: 13 July 2026". Announced 10 September 2026 with an author survey: https://corp.oup.com/news/supporting-researchers-to-disclose-ai-use-with-confidence/ (VERIFIED, datePublished 2026-09-10).
- Precedence:
> "In the event of any variation between this policy and the journal's specific guidance, authors should follow the journal's specific guidance."
> "You will be required to assert upon submission of your article in your journal's submission system that you have read and adhered to this policy and any journal specific policy variations."
- Permitted **without** disclosure:
> "Improving clarity of language (e.g. spelling or grammar correction)"
> "Formatting references to comply with journal style requirements (authors must ensure that all references are accurate and valid with no hallucinations)"
- Typically permitted **with** disclosure: substantial editing/rewriting/reorganizing; translation; "Collecting, organizing, formatting, analyzing, and/or visualizing data using AI"; "Generating or refining code using AI"; "Generating illustrative or explanatory images, such as graphical abstracts, using AI"; derivative figures from published works if third-party rules are met.
- **Not** permitted:
> "Creating content by Gen AI Tools and misrepresenting as though it is original research data/results created by humans"
> "Creating or altering primary factual, scientific, or technical images, such as clinical or diagnostic images, documentary images, or images of real people, objects, or events"
- Where:
> "Any use of Gen AI Tools should be disclosed both in your cover letter to editors and in the Acknowledgements section of manuscripts. You may also be required to disclose specific uses in the Methodology section of your manuscript where the use of Gen AI Tools was integral to your research methodology. ... This disclosure must be made at initial submission and updated as relevant for any revision(s) submitted."
- What (required): "Name and version of the Gen AI Tool used"; "A description of how the Gen AI Tool was used"; "The author's role in directing and reviewing the work generated by the Gen AI Tool to ensure accuracy of the output". Strongly recommended for complex cases: "Date(s) of use"; "Specific sections of the document where the Gen AI Tool was used".
- Consequences:
> "Failure to disclose use of Gen AI Tools may be grounds for rejection."
> "These actions can include corrections or in some cases retractions to address any form of misrepresentation, including the undisclosed involvement of Gen AI Tools."

OUP journal-wide authorship clause (also on the Bioinformatics, NAR and BiB pages), VERIFIED:
> "Natural language processing tools driven by artificial intelligence (AI) do not qualify as authors, and the Journal will screen for them in author lists. The use of AI (for example, to help generate content or images, write code, process data, or for translation) should be disclosed both in cover letters to editors and in the Methods or Acknowledgements section of manuscripts."

Note: OUP's *book* policy (written permission needed for AI-generated content) is a different document and does not apply to journals.

### 2.2 *Bioinformatics* (OUP, ISCB) — VERIFIED

- URL: https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/pages/author-guidelines (no date shown; © 2026). It adopts the ISCB policy, which overrides the OUP default where they differ.
- ISCB policy: https://transition.iscb.org/iscb-policy-statements/iscb-policy-for-acceptable-use-of-large-language-models, "Updated 3 April 2025". It says OUP adopted it for *Bioinformatics*.

Verbatim from the *Bioinformatics* guidelines, "Policy for Acceptable Use of Large Language Models":
> Common acceptable uses: "As an aid to correct written text (spell checkers, grammar checkers)"; "As an aid to language translation, however, the researcher is responsible for the accuracy of the final text"; "As an algorithmic technique for research study"; "As an evaluation technique (to assist in finding inconsistencies or other anomalies)"; "Assist in code writing, however, the researcher is responsible for the correct code"; "Create documentation for code, however, the researcher is responsible for the correct documentation".
> "It is permissible to include LLM generated text snippets as examples in research papers where appropriate, but these MUST be clearly labelled, and their use explained."
> "Any acceptable use of LLMs or related technologies to produce, or help to produce, part of the text, figures or other contents of the paper should be explicitly declared and documented with sufficient details in the supplementary materials."
> Unacceptable: "It is not acceptable to use LLMs or related technologies to draft papers (including but not limited to text, figures, tables, and references) from a prompt text. In essence, papers must be written by researchers."
> "LLMs cannot be listed as authors as they would not fulfil the requirements of authorship as laid out in the ICMJE guidelines."
> "It is not acceptable to submit manuscripts received for review to an LLM, nor to rely on an LLM or related technology for generating the review."

The ISCB page itself adds:
> "To discover background information on a topic, subject to verification from trusted sources." (acceptable)
> "At present, we do not intend to systematically detect usage of these models, but we will investigate reported instances on a case-by-case basis."

Verifiability and desk-rejection rules on the same *Bioinformatics* page (VERIFIED):
- Software: "provide a self-contained and easy-to-use implementation of the software together with appropriate test data and detailed documentation on how to acquire, install, and run the software. This includes information on the purpose and values of all optional parameters."; "make the software and the source code freely available at stable URLs (e.g. github, bitbucket) without request to non-commercial users at the time of submission"; "ensure that the submitted software version and test data are also archived at dedicated repositories such as Zenodo, Figshare, Software Heritage, CRAN, and Bioconductor and provide the corresponding URL in the availability section of the abstract."; "We require authors to provide information on how to reproduce results/figures described in the manuscript."; "If the study relies on already published methods, the corresponding software used must have its code open and accessible to allow for reproducibility of the results described in the manuscript."
- Application Notes: "Software or data must be freely available to non-commercial users. ... Authors must also ensure that the software is available for a full two years following publication. Web services must not require mandatory registration by the user." "The name of the application should be included in the title."
- Web servers: accessible "without login credentials", "fully functional at the time of submission", HTTPS, test data, source code at stable URLs, "provide support / maintenance for at least 3 years after publication".
- Machine learning: "Papers using machine learning must contain a dedicated subsection clearly describing the composition of the training dataset." ... "how homology between sequences is taken into account" ... "**Papers using leave-one-out will be editorially rejected** unless there is a special circumstance" ... "Machine learning papers must report the performance on an independent test set. It is not sufficient to report the average error over the individual cross-validation sets."
- Data: "The inclusion of a data availability statement is a requirement for papers published in the Journal."
- Real data: "Actual biological data must be used ... Only in extremely well-justified cases can simulated data alone be used instead of real data."
- Pre-screening: "Papers that are considered to be of less significance to the readership of the journal are returned without review." (~30% acceptance)
- Length: "manuscripts that exceed the limit by 20% or more are usually returned by the Editorial Office immediately" (Original Paper 7 pages ≈ 5,000 words; Application Note 4 pages ≈ 2,600 words or 2,000 + one figure).
- Abstract: headings "Motivation, Results, Availability and Implementation, Contact and Supplementary Information"; "A maximum of 150 words is recommended." Application Notes: "Summary, Availability and Implementation, Contact and Supplementary Information".
- Section-specific: "New methods MUST be compared to existing state-of-the-art methods"; "We will not consider manuscripts that describe straightforward application of established computational methods to biological datasets, nor those that report incremental improvements in performance on benchmark datasets without a substantial advance in methodology."

## 3. Springer Nature, Nature Portfolio, BMC

### 3.1 Springer Nature risk framework (Green/Amber/Red) — VERIFIED

- Nature Portfolio: https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai (no date on page). Group page: https://group.springernature.com/gp/group/ai/ai-guidance-for-our-researchers-and-communities. Author page: https://www.springernature.com/gp/policies/editorial-policies/ai-manuscript-preparation.
- Announced **10 September 2026** (Research Information, https://www.researchinformation.info/news/springer-nature-launches-framework-on-use-of-artificial-intelligence/, datePublished 2026-09-10; STM Publishing News 16 Sep 2026). The same day as the OUP announcement.

Verbatim (nature.com):
> "Human accountability is non‑transferable — Accountability for scholarly content, evaluation, and editorial decisions cannot be delegated to AI systems."
> "AI may support, but must not replace, scholarly judgement — AI can assist clarity, efficiency, and exploration, but must not determine conclusions, evaluations, or decisions."
> "Confidentiality and data protection are mandatory — Manuscripts, peer review reports, and sensitive data must not be shared with unsecured or public AI systems."
> "Use of AI is governed according to risk level, not by tool type. The same framework applies across all roles: authors, reviewers and editors."
- **Green, "Permitted: Assistive AI use"**: "Using an LLM to polish or refine the language"; "Suggesting structure or formatting of manuscript sections"; "Translation, structuring or clarifying reviewer comments". "Permitted. Disclosure enhances trust and transparency and clearly demonstrates human accountability."
- **Amber, "Exercise caution: Evaluative or interpretive AI use"**: "Suggesting analytical, experimental or methodological approaches"; "Comparing results to existing literature"; "Extensive copy editing or writing support"; "Pattern identification in exploratory data analysis"; "Explaining outputs from statistical models in plain language"; "Recommending statistical tests or modelling approaches". "Permitted with human oversight, verification, and transparency through disclosure."
- **Red, "Not permitted: AI replacing scholarly judgement or lacking transparency"**: "AI use is opaque or creates non‑verifiable outputs"; "Generating hypotheses, analyses or conclusions and presenting them as human-derived"; "Using an LLM to generate core research reasoning without disclosure"; "Assigning authorship or accountability to AI systems or tools"; "Creating photorealistic images (deepfakes)". The BMC copy adds "Delegating peer review to an LLM".
- Reviewers: "May use AI to support peer review but must not upload manuscript content to unsecured or public AI tools" (secure or institutionally approved tools allowed). This is a change from the older blanket ban, which still survives in older BMC peer-review text.

Author page (springernature.com, ai-manuscript-preparation), VERIFIED:
> "More extensive uses, such as drafting or restructuring content, require greater care. Authors should ensure that: disciplinary conventions are correctly applied; references are real and have been checked; the intellectual contribution remains their own"
> "AI generated visual content that is not derived from independently verifiable data, source material, methods, computational outputs or author-developed content is considered opaque and is not permitted by Springer Nature policy."
> "Where AI-generated or AI-assisted visual content is published, information about the AI system used, the purpose of its use, and the extent of its contribution should be provided in an AI Declaration in your manuscript. Specific disclosure should be provided within the caption or legend of the visual content."
> "Declaration does not negatively influence editorial decisions"

Inconsistencies inside Springer Nature's own pages (a checker should flag both and follow the journal's page):
- The group page says "The use of generative AI tools should be declared in the Introduction or Acknowledgements of the manuscript. The use of an AI tool for 'AI assisted copy editing' purposes does not need to be declared." Its FAQ on the same page says LLM use "must be documented in the manuscript's Methods section (or equivalent)", and that AI copy-editing "should be declared in line with our AI guidance".
- The group FAQ still says of generative AI images: "Generally no". The new author page allows AI visuals derived from verifiable inputs.
- A group-page block titled "How can authors use AIGC?" asks for "tool versions, usage dates, prompts, and the extent of AI contribution". It reads like region-specific guidance; its scope is unclear.

## 4. Other publishers

### 4.1 Elsevier — VERIFIED ("Policy updated June 2026")

- https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals (page footer: "Policy updated June 2026."). Announced 18 Aug 2026: https://www.elsevier.com/connect/updated-generative-ai-policies-for-journals-supporting-responsible-use-while-protecting-trust
> "Authors should disclose the use of AI tools for manuscript preparation in a separate AI declaration statement included in their manuscript upon submission. This statement will appear in the published article ... Authors should document their use of AI, including the name of the AI tool used, the purpose of its use, and the extent of their oversight."
> "Basic checks of grammar, spelling and punctuation do not need a declaration statement. However, when an AI tool makes substantive changes to sentence structure or organization of a part of the text, this should be disclosed."

## 6. Synthesis: checkable requirements for a manuscript checker

Rule IDs are suggestions. "B" = *Bioinformatics*-specific.

### 6.1 Authorship and attribution (universal)
- A1. No AI tool among authors or in the CRediT list (all bodies). *Bioinformatics*: "the Journal will screen for them in author lists".
- A2. No AI tool cited as an author or as a primary source (ICMJE V.A; Science: cited sources may not be AI-authored; Sage). Elsevier and GigaScience differ: GigaScience *requires* in-text citations and reference-list entries for AI-writing tools. Follow the journal.

### 6.2 Disclosure (presence, placement, content)
- D1. Check whether the current target requires a disclosure for the actual use. The dated notes below describe different exemptions; they are not interchangeable. A missing statement without known author use and an applicable rule is not a violation. Distinguish language correction, substantive drafting and research use.
- D2. Placement:
  - *Bioinformatics*/BiB: cover letter + Methods or Acknowledgements + **supplementary materials documentation**.
  - OUP default: cover letter + Acknowledgements (+ Methodology if integral), updated at each revision.
  - Elsevier/Cell: separate titled declaration (Elsevier: before references; Cell: after Declaration of interests).
  - Wiley: Acknowledgments / Methods / figure captions.
  - JAMA: Acknowledgment (Methods for research).
  - IEEE: acknowledgments, with sections named.
  - Science: cover letter + Methods or Acknowledgments.
  - MDPI: submission system + Methods + Acknowledgments.
  - eLife: Materials and Methods.
  - BMJ: contributor section (+ Methods).
- D3. Content fields: tool name **and version** (OUP, T&F, Frontiers, Science, JAMA, Wiley, MDPI, Elsevier figures); purpose/how used; sections affected (IEEE required; OUP recommended); author's verification role (OUP, PLOS, Wiley); dates (JAMA required; Wiley, OUP recommended); manufacturer (JAMA, Elsevier research use). Prompts: ICMJE "where applicable" for research use, WAME, Frontiers (encouraged), Wiley (on request).
- D4. Cover letter mentions AI use (ICMJE, OUP, *Bioinformatics*, Science).
- D5. AI used for **code** that changes functionality, pipelines or models is described in Methods with model and version (Science, Wiley, Elsevier, ACM, ICMJE). Matters for a codebase built with coding agents.

### 6.3 Prohibited content
- P1 (B). The dated Bioinformatics/ISCB notes restrict AI drafting. The current ISCB page fetched on 2026-10-15 refers to drafting paper sections. Verify the target journal's wording; style signals do not establish drafting history. Explain a material conflict neutrally and follow the author's decision rather than treating the external policy as an assistant prohibition.
- P2. Leftover LLM residue: "Here is…", "Would you like me to…", "As an AI…", "[insert]", "illustrative data, replace with real numbers", unreplaced placeholders, TODOs. arXiv one-year-ban trigger; NeurIPS calls it "AI residue".
- P3. AI-generated or AI-altered primary research images forbidden (OUP, Elsevier, Cell, Wiley, T&F, JAMA, Springer Nature opaque visuals). Science and eLife (striking image) forbid AI illustrative images too. General-purpose gen-AI graphical abstracts forbidden at Elsevier/Cell. AI-assisted data visualizations must be reproducible from data and code and disclosed in the legend.
- P4. AI-generated data presented as real results (all). Synthetic data replacing missing data without methodology (T&F).
- P5. Responses to reviewers not AI-generated (JAMA; Science bars entering reviews or the manuscript into an LLM during revision).

### 6.4 References (the clearest trigger for desk rejection or retraction)
- R1. Verify reference identity against an authoritative record, with a DOI/PMID where available. A genuine work need not have either identifier, and a failed lookup is not proof of fabrication. Treat an identity mismatch or a confirmed nonexistent work separately from missing metadata or inaccessible sources.
- R2. Each cited source supports its sentence (ICMJE "attest that the references cited support the associated statement"; SN "Have read all cited sources"; WAME).
- R3. No retracted works cited except about the retraction (ICMJE: check PubMed; SN "improper citations to retracted works").
- R4. Cite original works, not derivations or reviews (SN). Include DOIs where possible (SN/BMC).
- R5. Data and software cited with persistent identifiers (ICMJE "Published articles should reference the unique, persistent identifiers of the data sets employed"; OUP; GigaScience FORCE11).
- R6. JAMA: references not AI-generated or AI-formatted. IEEE: exclude references from AI editing.

### 6.5 Verifiability (*Bioinformatics* and peers)
- V1 (B). Source code public at a stable URL at submission, no request needed; archived version (Zenodo, Figshare, Software Heritage, CRAN, Bioconductor) URL in the abstract's Availability and Implementation section.
- V2 (B). Test data and install/run documentation including all optional parameters; instructions to reproduce each result and figure.
- V3 (B). Software of relied-upon published methods also open.
- V4 (B). Application Note: availability ≥2 years; no mandatory registration. Web server: HTTPS, no login, test data, help/tutorial, ≥3 years maintenance. NAR: ≥5 years, Zenodo/FigShare DOI in Data Availability.
- V5 (B). Data availability statement present with links or identifiers.
- V6 (B). ML: training-set composition subsection, independent test set, homology handling, no leave-one-out (editorial rejection).
- V7 (B). New methods compared against state of the art on real data; simulated-only data needs strong justification.
