## Generative AI

AI-based tools and technologies for content generation

If you have used AI tools (e.g. large language models, generative AI, and chatbots) in your research or the writing of your article, please note the following authorship principles:

You must not list AI tools as a co-author of your article . This is because authorship requires taking accountability for content, consenting to publication via a publishing agreement, and giving contractual assurances about the integrity of the work. These are uniquely human responsibilities that cannot be undertaken by AI tools.

You must clearly acknowledge within your article use of Generative AI tools . Please add a statement in the Methods or Acknowledgments section which includes:

Use of AI tools to assist copyediting and language refinement is permitted. Authors must retain the original and revised versions of the article, which must be shared with the Editor upon request. A declaration from the author must be included within the submission stating:

the name and version number of the AI tool used;

confirmation the authors have checked terms of use for the specific AI tool used, and therefore confirm suitability for publication;

Use of AI tools to assist translation of authors own work is permitted. Authors must retain the original and translated versions of the article, which must be shared with the Editor upon request. If the article was previously published in another language, the author must ensure they have the appropriate rights to republish in another language before using any translation tool.

When you submit your work to a Taylor & Francis journal you may be asked to confirm that you have disclosed within your manuscript any use of AI tools. This level of transparency makes sure that editors and reviewers are aware that AI tools have been used, so that they can assess whether they have been used appropriately and responsibly.

Please see the Taylor & Francis AI policy for more details.

Any assistance from AI tools for content generation (e.g. large language models) and other similar types of technical tools which generate article content, must be clearly acknowledged within the article. If AI tools have been used to assist with translations of authors own work, any person(s) or organisation who assisted with the accuracy checks for the translation, but who does not meet authorship criteria, must be acknowledged.

It is the responsibility of authors to ensure the validity, originality and integrity of their article content. Authors are expected to use AI tools responsibly, transparently and in accordance with our editorial policies on authorship and principles of publishing ethics.

AI, Large Language Models (LLMs) and figures or images

We do not permit the use of Generative AI in the manipulation of images and figures, or original research data for use in our publications. The term "manipulation" includes augmenting, concealing, moving, removing, or introducing a specific feature within an image or figure. Use of Generative AI tools for creating or changing images related to the outputs of scientific and clinical research (e.g. images of outputs from in vitro and in vivo experiments, clinical samples and clinical diagnostic images) is strictly prohibited.

This includes images of outputs from in vitro and in vivo experiments, clinical samples and clinical diagnostic images. Use of Generative AI tools for creating or changing these types of images is strictly prohibited.

AI ethics and best practice

Artificial intelligence is evolving quickly, and additional applications for research are continually being developed. This means that guidelines for the use of AI in research are still evolving too. Please read our guidance on scenarios where use of AI tools in research and preparation of manuscripts may be permissible. on scenarios where use of AI tools in research and preparation of manuscripts may be permissible.

Please bookmark the Taylor & Francis AI policy to keep up to date with the latest guidance.

Key principles to be aware of when using AI in any part of the research process include:

AI technologies should always be used with human oversight and transparency .

You must receive permission from copyright holders prior to using copyrighted material in an AI tool. This applies, for example, when working on literature reviews and summaries.

Some potential uses of AI in research are considered unethical . For example, generative AI tools should not be used in ways that replace core researcher and author responsibilities.

You must also acknowledge the use of AI in any papers you write based on that research.

Above all, however you use AI, it remains your responsibility to make sure that your work meets the highest standards in your field of research.

Generative AI-assisted technologies such as large language models and image generators cannot qualify for authorship under our criteria and must not be listed as authors or co-authors on any submission.

See the Generative AI section for details of acceptable uses and reporting requirements.

PeerJ maintains editorial discretion to decline manuscripts if AI is used inappropriately. This includes cases where AI use compromises the integrity of the research, or where the extent of AI contribution raises concerns about the authors' contribution.
