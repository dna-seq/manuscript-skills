## Artificial Intelligence

We believe that artificial intelligence (AI) can support research and publishing when used responsibly, transparently, and with appropriate human oversight. AI can improve efficiency and assist with a range of activities across the research and publishing lifecycle, but it must not replace scholarly judgement, accountability, or editorial decision-making.

We achieve this by applying a risk-assessment framework across the research and publishing lifecycle. Specific guidance is available for AI use in manuscript preparation , AI use in research practice , AI use in peer review , and editor AI use and assessment guidance . Our editorial policies are guided by four core expectations: human accountability cannot be transferred to AI systems; AI may support but not replace scholarly judgement; transparency about AI use builds trust and confidence; and confidentiality and data protection must be maintained when using AI tools.

We recognise that AI technologies are evolving rapidly and will continue to review and update our policies to reflect developments in technology, regulation, and community expectations.
