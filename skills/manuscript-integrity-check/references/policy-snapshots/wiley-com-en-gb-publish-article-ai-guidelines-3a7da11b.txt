## Using AI tools in your research

## A guide for research authors, editors, and reviewers

Generative AI tools are rapidly transforming the research landscape, offering new ways to analyze data, refine writing, and accelerate discovery. Whether you're experimenting with these technologies or regularly integrating them into your work, using AI responsibly helps ensure your work remains original, ethical, and aligned with scholarly standards.

This guide is designed to support journal authors, editors, and peer reviewers across disciplines in making informed decisions about AI's role in research and writing. It outlines best practices, clarifies expectations around disclosure and oversight, and addresses common questions about using AI tools while safeguarding intellectual property rights and maintaining the integrity of your research.

These guidelines and FAQs apply to journal authors, editors, and peer reviewers. Book authors can find guidance in our AI writing guide for book authors .

Use AI responsibly: Apply best practices and properly disclose AI use in manuscripts and peer review

Navigate policies confidently: Understand copyright implications and publisher requirements for AI-generated content

Choose the right tools: Evaluate, test, and select appropriate AI technologies while recognizing and mitigating potential biases

Apply AI effectively: Leverage AI for literature reviews, manuscript drafting, data visualization, and research promotion while considering environmental sustainability

## Author guidance on generative AI tools

Authors may wish to use artificial intelligence tools or technologies ("AI Technology") when preparing a manuscript or other material for publication in journals published by Wiley (the "Material"). Wiley welcomes the thoughtful use of AI Technology. When used responsibly, authors can maintain high editorial standards, safeguard intellectual property and other rights, and foster transparency with readers. While Wiley authors remain fully accountable for their Material and any Technologies or sources they use in its creation, Wiley recognizes AI Technology's growing role in manuscript preparation and provides the following guidance.

## Review terms and conditions

Before choosing the AI Technology that can best support their research and manuscript development, authors should carefully review the terms and conditions, terms of use, or any other conditions or licenses associated with that technology. Authors must confirm that the AI Technology does not claim ownership of their content or impose limitations on its use, as this could interfere with an author's rights to use specific outputs in their manuscripts, the journal or an academic society's rights, or Wiley's rights, including Wiley's ability to publish the Material. Authors should periodically revisit the AI Technology terms to ensure continued compliance. For detailed guidance on evaluating AI tools for privacy and data protection, review the Protecting your content when using AI author FAQs .

## Human oversight

Authors may only use AI Technology as a companion to their writing process, not a replacement. As always, authors must take full responsibility for the accuracy of all content, and verify that all claims, citations, references, and analyses are aligned with their expertise and research. Before including AI-generated content in their Material, authors must carefully review it to ensure the final work reflects their expertise, voice, and originality while adhering to Wiley's ethical and editorial standards. For more information on how to maintain intellectual oversight while using AI Technology, review the Maintaining Intellectual Leadership with AI Technology author FAQs.

## Disclosure

Authors should maintain documentation of all AI Technology used, including its purpose, whether it impacted key arguments or conclusions, and how they personally reviewed and verified AI-generated content. Authors must disclose the use of AI Technologies when submitting their Material to a Wiley-published journal. If not provided, the journal may request this documentation. Transparency is essential to Wiley's commitment to integrity in publishing . For information on how to declare your use of AI Technologies, review the Disclosure and declaration of AI use author FAQs.

## Rights protection

Authors must not use any AI Technology that restricts their own, Wiley's, or any other party's use of the Material. This includes ensuring that the AI Technology used and the provider of that AI Technology does not gain any rights over the author's underlying content, including the right to ingest or "train" their AI Technology on the content, besides the limited right to access and use the Material to perform the service. By reviewing an AI Technology's terms and conditions for clauses such as "ownership", "data reuse", or "opt out" among others, authors can prevent unintended rights transfers. For further guidance on evaluating AI tools for rights protection, review the Content Rights and Permissions author FAQs.

## Responsible and ethical use

Authors must use AI Technology in a manner that aligns with privacy, confidentiality, and compliance obligations. This includes respecting data protection laws, avoiding the use of AI to replicate unique styles or voices of others, and fact-checking AI-generated content for accuracy and neutrality. Authors should be aware of potential biases in AI outputs and take steps to mitigate stereotypes or misinformation. When inputting sensitive or unpublished content, authors should use Technologies with appropriate privacy controls to protect confidentiality. For more information on responsible and ethical use of AI Technology, review the Protecting your content when using AI author FAQs and Bias and Equity Considerations author FAQs.

## Adherence to agreements

Wiley values authors' unique creativity and expertise and views AI Technologies as tools that enhance rather than replace creativity. Wiley remains committed to providing clear guidance that fosters trust with readers, protects authors' and Wiley's rights, and ensures high-quality content. These guidelines augment Wiley's Best practice guidelines on research integrity and publishing ethics and will evolve as technology and author needs advance.

## Maintaining intellectual leadership with AI Technology

Your expertise is what enables you to derive value from AI technology. Having foundational skills in the area where you want AI assistance helps you better direct the AI to produce useful output and to more efficiently evaluate AI output for quality. Consider your readiness across these key areas:

Directing AI effectively: Do you understand the underlying principles for the task at hand well enough that you can strategically direct the AI to support your work?

Evaluating AI outputs: Can you identify when AI outputs are inaccurate for your field or don't meet professional standards? Do you have time to validate output for areas where you aren't currently an expert?

## AI as a research assistant

Think of AI as a research assistant working under your direction, helping you accomplish your goals more efficiently. You're responsible for how you use AI to support your work, and accountable for the quality, accuracy, and integrity of your research and manuscript. When working with AI, your role is to: - Define your goals and expectations for your AI collaboration - Provide directions to the AI based on your specific needs - Evaluate outputs using your domain knowledge - Seek input from AI, then implement changes in the work yourself - Make final decisions about what to include in your work - Disclose your use of AI transparently in your manuscript so readers can understand your process

## AI for productivity and efficiency

AI can be an effective productivity tool for tasks like reference formatting, language polishing, data organization, and standard code generation. When using AI for efficiency, ask yourself: - Will this save me time and effort? - Does the output meet my standards for quality? - Am I confident I can verify and take responsibility for what the AI produces? - Does this application present potential integrity or ethical concerns?

## AI and critical thinking

Consider your intent before using AI. When using AI for tasks related to research or manuscripts, understanding how it can enhance or hinder your critical thinking helps you make strategic choices. Building expertise requires working through challenges, making mistakes, and problem-solving independently. These are things that AI cannot replace. AI can enhance your critical thinking when used to: - Support your initial learning journey for new topics or domains - Expand your thinking by generating multiple ideas or perspectives to evaluate - Identify and explore patterns across different studies or datasets - Challenge your assumptions and identify gaps or bias in thinking ‍ If you have concerns about becoming over reliant on AI, avoid: - Using AI to make decisions rather than to inform your judgment - Relying solely on AI summaries instead of engaging with key primary sources - Incorporating AI suggestions without understanding their rationale - Using AI to bypass skill-building opportunities

## Choosing when NOT to use AI

AI Technologies may not be right for every task or research project. Decide strategically where these tools genuinely support your research goals, and where established methods-the research approaches, tools, and techniques you currently use-may be more effective, accurate, or better aligned with professional standards.

You're working in an unfamiliar domain where verifying the AI outputs would take more time and effort than using established methods

Constraints or values make AI a poor fit

Institutional or funding requirements limit or restrict AI use

To make informed choices about when and how to use AI, it helps to understand the basics of how these tools work and what to look for when evaluating them. For more information on types of AI Technologies and their capabilities, see the Getting started with AI Technologies author FAQ.

## Disclosure and declaration of AI use

AI disclosure should be integrated into your manuscript using the same logic you apply to other tools and assistance. For journals published by Wiley, AI disclosure should be placed in the following sections of your manuscript:

In your Acknowledgments when AI assists with manuscript drafting, editing, translation, or formatting. In your Methods when AI is used to assist with your research methodology, data collection or analysis, or literature review processes. In your figure captions when AI generates or edits any visual content that appears in your manuscript.

DescriptionWiley requires disclosure of AI when used to generate substantial text or restructure arguments. Disclosure ensures transparency in manuscript evaluation and accountability by the authors for the content, a generally held authorship requirement. Simple grammar correction, formatting, and word choice suggestions typically do not require disclosure.

These examples demonstrate the distinction between AI assistance that creates or transforms content (requiring disclosure) versus AI that polishes existing work (typically no disclosure needed).

Translation: AI translates manuscript sections (e.g., abstract, methods) into another language

Cohesion and organization: AI restructures arguments or document flow; improves readability and flow of paragraphs; assists with transitions; reorganizes sections of the manuscript

## Content rights and permissions

Creators automatically retain copyright in their original work from the moment of creation. However, most jurisdictions set forth that copyright requires human authorship and does not allow AI to be considered an author. Where AI Technologies are used, there must be sufficient human contribution of creative, expressive elements in the final work in order to achieve copyright protection.

Wiley's agreements include AI-specific terms that hold authors responsible for the accuracy of any AI-assisted content and give journal editors final authority over whether AI use is appropriate.

Copyright infringement risks : As an author, you are responsible for ensuring that all content in your article can be legally published and is not subject to any restrictions on use by Wiley, pursuant to the author agreement. AI Technologies may inadvertently reproduce copyrighted material owned by third parties when generating text or images because they are often trained on copyrighted content. This creates a higher risk of copyright infringement and plagiarism than when AI is used to edit your existing work. Potential copyright infringement can be difficult to detect because the original source material may not be easily identifiable in AI outputs. Using copyrighted material without permission could result in mandatory revisions leading to publication delays, manuscript rejection, or retraction or even to legal claims for infringement.

Use AI Technologies that identify the sources of their outputs, then verify these sources are accurate and reliable

Avoid using AI Technology where the generated content is subject to "copyleft" licenses (like CC BY-SA or GNU General Public License)

When possible, use AI for editing and refining your own work rather than generating entirely new content

AI technology licensing / ownership restrictions: Authors should be aware that some AI Technologies do not grant full ownership of AI-generated content or may restrict reuse through various licensing arrangements. AI-generated content may be subject to "copyleft" licenses (like CC BY-SA or GNU General Public License) that require you to share your work under the same terms, or restrictive licenses that limit commercial use or require attribution.

When using AI Technology to support your manuscript creation:

Verify that the AI Technology allows broad reuse of outputs and places no restrictions on your commercial and publishing activities (e.g., no commercial, adaptation, copying, distribution, display, performance, geographic, or other restrictions) before inclusion in your manuscript

Review the AI Technology's licensing terms and check if attribution is required, ensure there is no restrictive license applied to the content (such as a CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC, or other licenses with restrictions on commercial and other reuse), and include any necessary attribution or modification details in your manuscript as required

As stated in Wiley's Best Practice Guidelines for Research Integrity and Publishing Ethics : "AI Technology cannot be considered capable of initiating an original piece of research without direction by humans. Tools cannot be accountable for published work or research design, which is a generally held requirement of authorship, nor do they have legal standing or the ability to hold or assign copyright. Therefore-in accordance with COPE's position statement on authorship and AI tools -these tools cannot fulfil the role of, nor be listed as, an author of an article." This means:

Your manuscript must demonstrate that the substance and expression of the work are yours, with AI serving only as assistant at your direction. Transparent disclosure of AI assistance shows readers and copyright authorities that you are the author, with AI Technologies serving only in a support role. For more information on Wiley's disclosure policies, see the Disclosure and declaration of AI use author FAQs.

Multi-authored papers involve shared copyright ownership, which means all co-authors must be able to truthfully claim authorship of the work. When AI Technologies are used, it's essential that all co-authors can account for any AI assistance in their collective work. We encourage transparency among co-authors about AI use in manuscript development and recommend discussing disclosure as a team. For detailed guidance on coordinating AI disclosure among co-authors and author contribution statements, see the How should co-authors coordinate disclosure of AI use author FAQ.

## Bias and equity considerations

AI Technologies do not have access to up-to-date subscription or paywalled journal content by default. As a result, they may overrepresent open access literature and general web content while underrepresenting research published in hybrid and subscription journals. To address this, look for AI tools that allow you to connect directly to your institutional subscription content through their interface. You should also supplement AI literature suggestions with searches in academic databases to discover paywalled content available through your institution.

Bias in AI is not only a technical flaw but an equity concern. Overreliance on skewed outputs can distort the scholarly record and perpetuate systemic inequities in research. While you can prompt AI for 'varied perspectives,' remember that it is limited by its training data and may not reliably correct its own bias. By critically evaluating AI and deliberately countering its biases, you help safeguard both the quality and impartiality of your scholarship.

AI Technologies do not typically have access to subscription or paywalled journal content. As a result, they may overrepresent open access literature while underrepresenting research published in hybrid and subscription journals. To address this, supplement AI literature suggestions with searches in academic databases to discover paywalled content available to you through your institution.

AI training data may overrepresent certain regions and cultural perspectives while underrepresenting others. This can narrow the range of voices in suggested sources. To broaden your perspective, supplement AI use with targeted database searches and consult international sources.

Large language models (LLMs) may reproduce harmful stereotypes or default to unfounded assumptions. This can affect how populations, regions, or research topics are represented in summaries and recommendations. Be alert to oversimplified or biased language in AI outputs, especially when describing people, cultures, or research practices.

AI outputs may emphasize dominant methodological approaches while underrepresenting less common or emerging traditions. If exploring new methods, consult journals focused on alternative approaches or colleagues from adjacent disciplines to broaden your view.

AI Technologies are not typically trained to prioritize environmental publications or sustainability considerations. As a result, recommended methods or study designs may overlook environmental implications or fail to suggest sustainable alternatives. To address this, actively prompt AI Technologies to consider environmental impact (for example, "What are the environmental considerations for this methodology?"), supplement with searches in sustainability-focused databases, and consult colleagues with expertise in environmental research methods or laboratory practices.

AI Technologies with fixed training cutoffs cannot access research published after their training date, which means recent literature will be underrepresented. Additionally, older foundational work may be less accessible if it wasn't well-represented in the AI's training data. To address this, use AI tools that connect to live databases or supplement with direct searches in academic databases. If your research requires historical context or foundational work from earlier decades, consult with librarians who specialize in archival research collections.

While AI Technologies may contain built-in biases, they can also help you reflect on your own biases as a researcher. All authors bring their experiences, disciplinary training, and assumptions to their work, which can shape how evidence is interpreted and presented. AI can help you identify gaps in reasoning, consider alternative viewpoints, and ultimately strengthen your research approach.

Try using AI to:

Challenge your arguments: Ask AI to challenge your research conclusions. "What are the strongest counterarguments to my findings?" or "What assumptions am I making that might be problematic?"

Identify gaps in your reasoning: Use AI to systematically review your argument structure. "Can you identify any logical gaps in this argument?" or "What evidence would be needed to strengthen this claim?"

Explore alternative perspectives: Prompt AI to consider different viewpoints on your research question. "How might researchers from different disciplines approach this problem?" or "What would critics of this approach argue?"

Test your methodology: Use AI to examine your research design critically. "What are the limitations of this methodology?" or "What alternative approaches might yield different insights?"

Remember that AI suggestions are starting points for your own critical thinking. AI cannot replace the diverse perspectives that come from collaborating with colleagues and peers from different backgrounds. Engage with your research network for the nuanced perspectives that AI cannot provide.

## Environmental considerations

The environmental impact of AI varies depending on the mode of use. Key examples include:

Retrieving external data (RAG): Linking AI to outside databases or live sources. This increases environmental impact because each query involves multiple retrievals and processing steps, often across different systems

Agentic use (autonomous workflows): Giving an AI system a goal and allowing it to carry out multiple steps independently. Because these workflows may involve long reasoning chains, repeated queries, and complex coordination, they can be more energy-intensive than standard inference

Training from scratch: Building or training a new model requires enormous computational resources, energy, and water. This is the most resource-intensive mode of AI use and is responsible for much of AI's reported carbon footprint

Being strategic about AI use helps you get better results while being environmentally conscious. The practical strategies below can help you get started.

Choose high-impact applications: Use AI when it meaningfully improves your work. Skip it for tasks you can do quickly and accurately with established Technologies. For example, a simple fact-check or literature search may be faster and lighter using your library's database tools than sending the same request to an AI model. Consider whether simple diagrams or existing imagery might serve your purpose before generating new visuals

Be strategic with built-in AI features: Many common tools (e.g., search engines, word processors) now include AI by default. Check whether you can turn off or limit these features in the settings menu. When you can't disable them entirely, choose the lowest-impact option. For example, use standard search instead of AI-generated overviews, or traditional spellcheck instead of AI rewriting

Match the tool to the task: General-purpose LLMs are the most common starting point for new AI users, but they are more resource-intensive than smaller, task-specific Technologies. Once you have a clearer sense of your specific use cases, consider adopting task-specific or domain-specific tools that run on smaller models. These lighter AI Technologies are often more efficient and can perform targeted tasks such as grammar checking, formatting citations, or data analysis with a much lower environmental impact. Consider which settings you enable for which tasks and avoid defaulting to the most advanced options unless they are necessary. For example, extended reasoning settings can improve accuracy when synthesizing findings across multiple papers, but for straightforward text editing or summarizing a short abstract, leaving this off will save energy without reducing quality

Understand task complexity levels: Just as summarizing many papers takes you more effort than summarizing one, the same can be said for AI. The more complex the task, the more computing resources and energy it requires

Different research tasks benefit from different levels of AI assistance. Understanding these categories helps you choose the most effective approach:

Running AI-assisted search, drafting short sections of text, generating code, summarizing short documents, converting tables between formats (for example: CSV to JSON to LaTeX)

## Navigating AI policies across institutions, funders, publishers, and journals

Research institutions, funding agencies, publishers, and journals may each impose restrictions on how AI Technologies can be used in research and publication.

Institutions: Many universities and research organizations issue AI policies that govern how their researchers may use these Technologies. If you're uncertain, consult your research office for clarification

Funders: Grant agreements may include specific requirements or restrictions on AI use in conducting or disseminating research. Always review your funding terms to ensure compliance

Publishers and journals: Editorial policies vary in what types of AI use they permit. If your use of AI does not align with a journal's policy, you may be asked to revise your manuscript to remove or rewrite AI-assisted sections. While this may feel inconvenient, disclosure and documentation brings transparency to the scholarly record

## How can I navigate conflicting AI guidance?

The landscape of AI guidance for authors is rapidly evolving as institutions, funders, and journals work to develop policies that balance innovation with upholding publication standards and research integrity. This can create uncertainty about how AI may be used in your work and what must be declared. Rather than trying to anticipate every possible requirement, focus on transparency and documentation. Maintaining clear records of your AI use allows you to adapt disclosures to meet different journal, funder, or institutional requirements without compromising integrity.

Review your institution's AI policy and note the date when you last checked it

Document your AI use consistently throughout your project so you can adapt disclosures as needed (AI Technology and version, month accessed, why it was used)

Always comply with the terms of your author agreement concerning AI and intellectual property and other matters

Over time, we expect policies to become clearer and more aligned as the research community gains experience with AI. In the meantime, this document is designed to help you navigate the current uncertainty with practical and actionable strategies.

## Is it safe to put my unpublished content into AI Technologies?

The question of content security and AI Technologies is complex and continuously evolving. You should carefully consider both the privacy implications and legal aspects of sharing content, as Technologies may have different terms regarding content rights and usage. Take extra caution when sharing potentially sensitive materials that may be retained by AI models.

When working with sensitive content, remove private details from text or data sets before using publicly available generative AI Technologies. Most publicly available Technologies have limited privacy controls, while professional or paid versions may include privacy settings to opt out of data training or to request data deletion. If your institution provides access to enterprise versions of AI, these offer the most robust privacy controls and are the safest option for handling sensitive data. Regardless of the AI Technology version, always prioritize those that explicitly state they do not retain or train on user data.

## AI in peer review

## Can I use AI to help me review manuscripts?

Authors' trust in peer review depends on maintaining strict confidentiality of unpublished work. Manuscripts under review are confidential and must not be uploaded to AI Technologies, whether in full or in part. Authors have not given permission for reviewers or editors to share their unpublished work with AI Technologies.

However, there are meaningful ways reviewers can use AI while protecting manuscript confidentiality.

Use AI to organize your thoughts and prioritize feedback

Use AI to improve review clarity and tone or translate your review

Ask AI general questions about research methods or statistical approaches without referencing manuscript specifics

Wiley is actively developing new approaches that will expand these possibilities while maintaining the confidentiality expected in review. See the What's next for AI in peer review author FAQ for more information.

## Do I need to disclose AI use to the author or editor?

We require disclosure of AI when it's used for drafting assistance in your reviews. When disclosing AI use in your review, we recommend sharing:

AI Technology's role in drafting or editing assistance

"GPT-5 was used to prioritize and polish review feedback. All original review feedback was provided by the peer reviewer, and all AI-generated content was reviewed, revised, and approved by the peer reviewer prior to sending this review."

## How should editors and reviewers respond to AI use in manuscripts?

At Wiley, AI use in manuscript development is not grounds for rejection. Editorial evaluation should focus on the quality, integrity, disclosure, and clarity of communication in the research, regardless of the Technologies used to support manuscript development.

We encourage authors to use AI responsibly when it helps them communicate their research more effectively and advance scientific discovery. Rather than asking "was AI used here?", the more important questions are: "Does this manuscript meet the editorial policies of my journal and the expectations of its readers? and "Are the disclosures regarding AI use consistent with what's presented in the manuscript?"

By responding to AI use with clarity and fairness, editors help create a culture that encourages transparency, supports innovation, and strengthens trust in the peer review process.

Start with author communication: If you notice issues that concern you, or concerns are raised to you by a peer reviewer-whether you suspect AI involvement or not-reach out to the author to discuss them directly. Focus your conversation on the specific problems you've identified and seek clarity from the author

## Getting started with AI Technologies

Testing AI Technologies before adopting them is essential for determining whether your chosen AI Technology supports your research needs without compromising your standards.

Because all AI Technologies behave and perform differently, we recommend trying multiple options using the checklist below. The goal is not to find a "perfect" Technology, but to identify Technologies that provide trustworthy support for your research without compromising your intellectual control or research standards. To learn more about the types of Technologies and capabilities currently available, see What types of AI Technologies are available for research authors? and What capabilities should I look for in AI Technologies?

Be aware that how you phrase your request to the AI (also called prompting) has a direct impact on the results you receive. Practice different ways to ask the AI to perform tasks and review how it responds. Look for tutorials on prompting strategies to understand how others are successfully using prompting techniques to interact with AI. When you begin testing AI Technologies, start with simple, low-risk tasks and increase complexity as your familiarity grows.

Start with familiar tasks: Begin by assigning the AI a task you can complete yourself. This helps you judge whether the AI Technology's performance is acceptable compared to your own standards

Compare multiple tools: Different Technologies may excel with different tasks. Comparing AI Technologies by running them through similar prompting exercises will help you understand their strengths, weaknesses, and unique features

1. Test accuracy in your field Ask the AI to explain 2-3 concepts from your research area.

2. Test source reliability Ask the AI to find papers and verify responses against primary sources.

☐ Are the links provided accurate and relevant? ☐ If citations are provided, are the authors and references real and accurate? ☐ Do citations support the claims as the AI suggests? ☐ Does the AI output over-represent certain voices or sources of information? ☐ Can the tool explain how it arrived at its results or which sources it used?

4. Test file format compatibility Upload non-sensitive documents-such as published papers, public datasets, or sample text from your field. Note: Be aware that AI Technology may train on or learn from any content you share.

☐ Does the AI support your most commonly used file types? ☐ When asked about the file, does the AI provide accurate responses?

5. Test output capabilities Ask the AI to provide example outputs for you to assess, for example, "create a table comparing X and Y" or "format this citation in APA style".

☐ How closely does the output match your request? ☐ Is the AI responsive to your feedback (critique, clarification, encouragement)? ☐ How many rounds of prompting are required to get the expected output?

6. Test practical limitations Work with the AI for an extended period to understand session constraints.

☐ Does the AI session last long enough for your typical research tasks? ☐ Does it retain context throughout the conversation? ☐ Can you manage conversation history (save, delete, restart)?

☐ Can you share outputs with colleagues in a usable format? ☐ Can you export or share AI conversation logs? ☐ Can the AI tool be used alongside your lab notebooks, reference managers, or other research tools?

8. Assess cost-benefit after testing Start testing by using free versions of AI Technology to understand capabilities. For the AI Technology you prefer, research what additional features are available in paid versions, which often provide better privacy controls and enhanced capabilities.

The "best" AI Technology depends on your specific research needs, institutional context, and personal workflow preferences. This framework helps you make systematic, informed decisions rather than following general recommendations.

There are many types of AI Technologies available to support research and writing tasks. This section will help you be aware of the options available so you can explore what works best for different tasks. You don't need to have an in-depth understanding of these options to get started. Most authors start with a basic LLM and gradually explore more specialized options as their needs become clearer.

General purpose Large Language Models

LLMs are versatile, conversational AI Technologies trained on vast datasets. They enable users to ask questions, generate ideas, and refine content through interactive dialogue. Most LLMs aren't trained specifically for research tasks or with research data, so their output may be incomplete or out of date. Because they can generate convincing but inaccurate information, always verify factual claims and citations against trusted sources.

Task-specific AI Technologies

These AI Technologies are designed for defined functions such as citation management, plagiarism detection, transcription and note taking, or grammar checking. Because they are built for narrow purposes, they often outperform general LLMs in accuracy and usability. Many also offer guided workflows and integration with existing research software, making them accessible for authors without AI expertise.

Domain-specific scientific AI Technologies

These AI Technologies are trained on scientific data or designed with embedded subject knowledge, making them more suitable for technical research applications. They may support experimental design, data analysis, lab management, molecular modeling, protein structure prediction, or visualization of complex systems. These AI Technologies require domain knowledge to use effectively.

This category is rapidly evolving, and availability varies significantly by research domain. In some cases, researchers are also developing custom AI Technologies where no commercial solution exists.

AI-integrated features in established research tools

Many research tools now include built-in AI features that help with writing, visualization, or project management. Because they are embedded within trusted platforms, these features often offer stronger privacy controls and institutional compliance. They can also offer a low-friction way to experiment with AI without adopting an entirely new software platform.

As AI Technologies evolve, so do their capabilities. Advanced features such as extended reasoning, real-time tool access, and independent decision-making are enabling researchers and authors to use AI in new ways. Practical capabilities like source transparency and workflow compatibility address common pain points and make AI easier to use effectively. These capabilities can appear across any of the AI technologies described above-from basic LLMs to specialized technologies for research purposes.

AI Technologies with extended reasoning are designed to help you think through complex problems in structured ways. They can break down multifaceted research questions in systematic analytical steps, suggest methodological frameworks or research design elements, and help organize complex arguments. They can effectively process and compare multiple research papers, datasets, or documents simultaneously to support thematic analysis or identify gaps. You may have heard these referred to as: reasoning models, advanced reasoning, deep reasoning, or chain-of-thought.

AI Technologies with this capability act more like thinking partners than assistants. They excel at systematic analysis, while you provide domain expertise, contextual judgment, and final decision-making authority.

Some AI platforms can interact with live sources such as search engines, APIs, code environments, or databases. Rather than relying solely on pre-trained knowledge, these AI Technologies retrieve current and source-verifiable data in real time. You may have heard these referred to as: web-enabled AI, RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation), tool calling, or live data access.

AI Technologies with independent decision-making capabilities can work through complex research tasks without requiring you to direct each step. Instead of prompting at every stage, you can set a research goal and allow the AI to work systematically toward it while you focus on other tasks. You may have heard these referred to as: agentic AI, autonomous agents, or agent workflows.

Some AI Technologies provide links to primary sources, inline citations, or a list of reference materials. This allows you to quickly verify claims, trace ideas back to original works, and identify when outputs may over-rely on certain sources.

Source transparency strengthens academic rigor by giving you clear visibility into the evidence behind AI outputs and helping you verify sources more efficiently. For more detailed guidance on using AI responsibly in literature reviews, see the Literature review and AI use author FAQ.

AI Technologies are most effective when they integrate smoothly into your existing research tools and practices. Workflow-compatible tools reduce friction, support collaboration, and let you spend more time focusing on the research itself rather than copying, reformatting, or troubleshooting.

Workflow compatibility ensures AI supports your research without disrupting your established processes.

## Methods and AI use in developing this guidance

This guidance and all author FAQs were developed through direct engagement with authors and editors. We conducted in-depth interviews with over 40 authors and editors across various disciplines to understand how they are incorporating AI Technologies into their work. These conversations revealed nuanced insights that shaped our guidance and highlighted key questions that authors have about confidently and safely applying AI Technologies in their manuscript development.

Generative AI use in research analysis and content development Interviews were recorded and transcribed utilizing Copilot in Microsoft Teams (February 2025 - June 2025). Interview notes were entered and tagged manually by the researchers in Miro (June - July 2025) and then sorted using the clustering feature. All interviews were either directly conducted by or reviewed by the primary author of this work to ensure familiarity with the interview content prior to introducing generative AI Technologies for analysis and drafting.

The drafting authors directed content development through extensive collaboration with Claude 4 Sonnet (July 2025). All content shared with the AI Technology was de-identified to ensure interviewee confidentiality. Notes and direct quotes were shared with Claude 4 Sonnet one theme at a time, along with prior Wiley AI guidance as context for the desired output format and content structure. The drafting authors provided editorial direction for initial section drafts, which were composed by Claude 4 Sonnet based on interview findings. Through iterative rounds of review and revision, all text content was refined and rewritten by the author to ensure accuracy, appropriate tone, and alignment with Wiley's vision for supporting authors in responsible AI use.

The drafting authors used Claude 4 Sonnet and GPT-5 (July - August 2025) for comprehensive document review to identify potential gaps in flow, consistency, and completeness. All AI recommendations were evaluated and selectively incorporated based on the research team's editorial judgment and subject matter expertise. All AI-generated content was verified against original interview data to ensure accuracy and authentic representation of researcher insights.
