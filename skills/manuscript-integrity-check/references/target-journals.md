# Generative-AI / LLM policies of candidate venues (checked 2026-10-15)

> Researched 2026-10-15 for the `manuscript-integrity-check` skill: candidate bioinformatics and genomics venues not covered in `publisher-policies.md`. Re-fetch a venue's page before relying on it.

Use these as dated evidence notes, not a checklist to combine across journals. Follow
`author-priorities.md` and distinguish checkable requirements from process claims with
`requirement-checkability.md`. An unresolved policy interpretation is the author's decision.

## How this was read

- Status marks:
  - **VERIFIED**: I read the venue's or publisher's own page.
  - **PARTIAL**: I read part of the primary material, but the live journal page could not be read in full.
  - **SECONDARY**: the information comes from news reports or third parties.
  - **NOT FOUND**: I searched and found nothing.
- Fetch method. Most pages were read through headless Chrome (`--dump-dom`, then `w3m -dump`), so the quotes are exact text.
- OUP pages (academic.oup.com) sit behind a Cloudflare Turnstile that blocked headless Chrome and the DevTools route. They were read two ways:
  - WebFetch, which gets through but returns only quoted fragments of 125 characters or less;
  - dated Wayback Machine snapshots.
  Each OUP item says which route was used.
- Page copies were kept only for the research session; re-fetch the URLs to re-check.
- Most pages show no "last updated" date. Where a date is shown, it is quoted.

## 1. Genome Research (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory Press): VERIFIED

**URLs**
- Journal section: https://genome.cshlp.org/ifora_Artificial (linked from https://genome.cshlp.org/ifora)
- Press-wide policy: https://cshjournals.org/editorial_policies, section "Artificial Intelligence (AI)"

**Page date:** none shown (footer "Copyright © 2026").

**Verbatim, journal page ("Use of artificial intelligence tools and large language models"):**
> "Authors are accountable for the accuracy, integrity, and originality of their work, including content generated by artificial intelligence (AI) tools, large language models (LLMs), and similar technologies."
> "The use of AI tools or LLMs to generate article content should instead be fully documented in the Methods section of the manuscript. This includes reporting the AI tool or LLM and version used, with a complete description of how it was used. It is not necessary to acknowledge the use of AI-based grammar/spelling checkers or reference managers."

**Verbatim, CSHL Press general policy:**
> "CSHL Press adheres to the ICMJE guidelines on the use of artificial intelligence in publishing"
> "the use of AI tools, including LLMs, to generate article content, or for data collection, analysis, or figure preparation (see below), and/or for anything that can impact methodological soundness, data integrity, or reproducibility of the submitted work, should be fully documented in the Methods section of the manuscript. This includes reporting the generative AI tool or LLM and version used, with a complete description of how it was used. Nondisclosure of AI use may require corrective action and may be construed as misconduct in some circumstances, per ICMJE guidelines. Please note, however, that declaring the use of AI-assisted technologies for refining, correcting, editing, or formatting a manuscript to improve clarity of language, is not required."
> "The use of AI-generated or AI-edited graphical assets is strongly discouraged."
> "Manuscripts submitted to CSHL Press journals are screened for plagiarism." ... "CSHL Press reserves the right to employ automated tools to identify any instances of plagiarism and withdraw the contribution if this is detected." ... "Editors may use software to screen images for manipulation."

**Answers**
1. **Writing help:** allowed. Generating content requires disclosure. Refining, correcting, editing or formatting for language clarity needs no disclosure, and neither do grammar/spelling checkers. Refining is clearly distinguished from generating.
2. **Disclosure:** in the Methods section. Give the tool or LLM, its version, and a complete description of use. No cover-letter, prompt or date requirement is stated.
3. **Screening:** plagiarism screening, "automated tools" for plagiarism, and image-screening software. No AI-text detector is named.
4. **Penalties:** nondisclosure "may require corrective action and may be construed as misconduct". A contribution may be withdrawn if plagiarism is detected.
5. **Code / software:** nothing AI-specific. AI use that affects "methodological soundness, data integrity, or reproducibility" must go in Methods; reading that as covering AI-written analysis code is my interpretation. The general rule: "should be prepared to make that software freely available for academic and nonprofit use".
6. **Preprints:** allowed ("posting unrefereed manuscripts on preprint servers will not be considered prior publication").

## 2. Database: The Journal of Biological Databases and Curation (OUP): PARTIAL

**URLs**
- https://academic.oup.com/database/pages/instructions_for_authors (hub: /database/pages/General_Instructions)
- OUP default policy: https://academic.oup.com/pages/for-authors/journals/preparing-and-submitting-your-manuscript/ai-principles-for-oxford-journals/policy-on-ai-use-and-disclosure-for-oxford-journals-authors

**What was readable**
- **Live page:** WebFetch returned only the first ~10.7k characters (submission, aims, open-access licence, APC calculator) before truncation. No AI text appears in that part.
- **Latest full Wayback snapshot (2025-04-18):** no AI, LLM or ISCB text at all. The only screening statement is: "Manuscripts submitted may be screened with iThenticate anti-plagiarism software in an attempt to detect and prevent plagiarism."
- **2026-04-20 snapshot of the hub page:** shows the society banner "International Society for Biocuration". Database is the ISB's journal, not ISCB's.

**Does it adopt the ISCB policy?**
NOT FOUND: there is no evidence that Database adopts ISCB's LLM policy. The ISCB text appears on *Bioinformatics*' page ("The International Society for Computational Biology (ISCB) has created an acceptable use policy for large language models (LLMs), which the journal follows." — Wayback snapshot 2026-08-17). The equivalent text is absent from every Database page I could read. The OUP default therefore applies (inference). OUP says "individual journals may have custom requirements, in addition to those stated here."

**OUP default (VERIFIED via WebFetch; "Policy last updated on: 13 July 2026"). Verbatim fragments:**
- Permitted without disclosure: "Improving clarity of language (e.g. spelling or grammar correction)"; "Formatting references to comply with journal style requirements ..."
- Typically permitted with disclosure: "Substantially editing, rewriting, or reorganizing text using AI"; "Translating text using AI"; "Generating or refining code using AI"; "Collecting, organizing, formatting, analyzing, and/or visualizing data using AI"
- Not permitted: "Creating content by Gen AI Tools and misrepresenting as though it is original research data/results created by humans"
- Where to disclose: "Any use of Gen AI Tools should be disclosed both in your cover letter to editors and in the" / "Acknowledgements section of manuscripts." / "You may also be required to disclose specific uses in the Methodology section of your manuscript" / "This disclosure must be made at initial submission and updated as relevant for any revision(s) submitted."
- What to state: "Name and version of the Gen AI Tool used"; "A description of how the Gen AI Tool was used (e.g. substantial editing or text generation,"; "The author's role in directing and reviewing the work generated by the Gen AI Tool to ensure accuracy of the output". For complex cases, strongly recommended: "Date(s) of use" and "Specific sections of the document where the Gen AI Tool was used"
- Consequences: "Failure to disclose use of Gen AI Tools may be grounds for rejection." / "These actions can include corrections or in some cases retractions to address any form of misrepresentation," / "including the undisclosed involvement of Gen AI Tools."

**Answers (OUP default)**
1. **Writing help:** allowed with disclosure. Light language correction is exempt. "Substantial editing or text generation" must be disclosed. The policy never uses the word "drafting".
2. **Disclosure:** cover letter plus Acknowledgements, and possibly Methodology. State the tool and version, how it was used, and the author's oversight role; dates and sections are recommended. Disclose at submission and update at revision. Prompts are not mentioned.
3. **Screening:** the OUP AI policy names no AI-text detector. The 2025 Database page names iThenticate (plagiarism).
4. **Penalties:** grounds for rejection; corrections or retractions.
5. **Code:** "Generating or refining code using AI" is permitted with disclosure.

For contrast, the stricter *Bioinformatics*/ISCB rule (Wayback snapshot 2026-08-17): "It is not acceptable to use LLMs or related technologies to draft papers (including but not limited to text, figures, tables, and references) from a prompt text. In essence, papers must be written by researchers." Disclosure there goes "in the supplementary materials", and also "in cover letters to editors and in the Methods or Acknowledgements section".

## 3. Scientific Data (Springer Nature): VERIFIED

**URLs**
- https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/editorial-and-publishing-policies
- Journal AI page: https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/ai
- Portfolio page: https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai
- SN manuscript-preparation guidance: https://www.springernature.com/gp/policies/editorial-policies/ai-manuscript-preparation

**Page date:** none shown.

**Journal-specific deviation:** the Scientific Data AI page is word-for-word the Nature Portfolio framework (I diffed the two bodies: identical). The only Scientific Data-specific text is an older sentence on the authorship page:
> "Large Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria. ... Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the Methods section (and if a Methods section is not available, in a suitable alternative part) of the manuscript."

The new framework itself no longer names a section; the SN guidance asks for an "AI Declaration in your manuscript".

**Verbatim, SN 2026 risk-assessment framework (Green / Amber / Red):**
- Green (low risk): "Using an LLM to polish or refine the language"; "Compliance and disclosure: Permitted. Disclosure enhances trust and transparency and clearly demonstrates human accountability."
- Amber: "Drafting explanatory summaries"; "Extensive copy editing or writing support"; "Permitted with human oversight, verification, and transparency through disclosure."
- Red (not permitted): "Fabricating data, citations or results"; "Using an LLM to generate core research reasoning without disclosure"; "Generating hypotheses, analyses or conclusions and presenting them as human-derived".
- Manuscript-preparation table:
  - Green ("Grammar and language editing; improving readability and flow; formatting; translation; summarising author-written text") → "Clearly describe use and confirm author accountability".
  - Amber ("Generating outlines; drafting sections based on author input; suggesting alternative phrasing ...") → same declaration, and "all AI generated or assisted content must be clearly labelled".
  - Red ("Writing results or discussion independently; generating scientific claims or interpretations; ... creating content from text prompts or without verifiable source data or material") → "Not permitted".

**Answers**
1. **Writing help:**
   - Allowed, with a declaration.
   - Polishing (Green) is distinguished from drafting sections from author input (Amber, allowed).
   - Prompt-only drafting, and independently written Results/Discussion (Red), are forbidden.
2. **Disclosure:** an "AI Declaration" in the manuscript describing the use and confirming author accountability. The Scientific Data authorship text still says Methods. For visuals: "Specific disclosure should be provided within the caption or legend". No prompt or date requirement is found.
3. **Screening:** see section 8 (SN-wide Geppetto and reference checker).
4. **Penalties:** none stated on the AI page itself ("Existing research integrity, ethics, and editorial governance processes apply"). See section 8 for citations.
5. **Code:**
   - Visuals must meet the requirement that "any AI-assisted processing, analysis or code generation has been subject to appropriate human review and validation".
   - Scientific Data requires a "Code availability" statement.
   - Nothing else is AI-specific.

## 5. iScience (Cell Press, Elsevier policy): VERIFIED

**URLs**
- https://www.cell.com/iscience/information-for-authors/journal-policies
- Elsevier: https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals ("Policy updated June 2026.")

**Journal-specific verbatim:**
> "Authors must declare the use of generative AI in scientific manuscript preparation upon submission of the paper"
> "Authors preparing a manuscript for submission can use generative AI tools, including agents and deep research tools, to synthesize complex literature, provide an overview of a field or research question, identify research gaps, generate ideas, and provide tailored support for tasks such as content organization and improving language and readability"
> "The statement will appear in the published work and should be placed in a new section after the "declaration of interests" section." Section title: "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process". Template: "During the preparation of this work, the author(s) used [NAME TOOL / SERVICE] in order to [REASON]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the published article."
> "The declaration does not apply to the use of basic tools, such as tools used to check grammar, spelling, and references. If you have nothing to disclose, you do not need to add a statement."
> "When the use of AI tools forms part of the research design or research methods, the use of the tools must be described in the manuscript in a reproducible manner in the methods or STAR Methods section and in the AI disclosure statement. This description should include the name of the model or tool, the version used, and the developer or manufacturer"
> Cell Press-specific: "General-purpose generative AI image tools may not be used in creation of graphical abstracts"; no AI cover art (except when integral to the research); no AI on research or data images.

2. **Disclosure:** a published declaration section after "Declaration of interests". Methods/STAR Methods (tool, version, developer) when AI is part of the methods. No prompts or dates required.
3. **Screening:** Crossref Similarity Check, cross-journal duplicate screening, image screening. No AI-text detector is named.
4. **Penalties:** fabricated references "may lead to rejection". Nothing else AI-specific.
5. **Code:** AI-written or AI-edited research code goes in Methods, held to the same reproducibility standard.

## Cross-venue notes

- **Drafting-ban gradient:**
  - Only the ISCB policy (*Bioinformatics*) forbids drafting a paper from a prompt outright.
  - Springer Nature forbids prompt-only content and AI-written Results/Discussion (Red), but allows "drafting sections based on author input" (Amber).
  - OUP, Elsevier/Cell Press, PLOS, T&F/F1000/PeerJ, CSHL, CSBJ/SPJ and GigaScience allow writing help with disclosure.
- **Language-polish exemptions:** CSHL/Genome Research, OUP, Elsevier/iScience and PLOS exempt it. PeerJ requires a declaration even for copyediting (and keeping both versions). CSBJ and GigaScience have no exemption.
- **Disclosure location:** varies by venue: Methods, Acknowledgements, a titled declaration, cover letter, submission form or supplement. Use only the actual target's required locations and exempt uses. One generic placement cannot be assumed to satisfy every journal, and repeating a statement in additional places is not a useful precaution by itself.
- **Named AI screening at submission:**
  - Springer Nature: Geppetto across its journals and books, plus the irrelevant-reference checker at almost all.
  - PLOS says it "may use available AI detection tools".
  - GigaScience "may use tools to detect undeclared or misuse of AI".
  - No other venue names an AI-text detector; they name only plagiarism and image tools: iThenticate, Crossref Similarity Check, ImageTwin, CrossCheck.
- **Hallucinated references are an explicit rejection/retraction ground:** BMC/SN citations policy and Elsevier.
